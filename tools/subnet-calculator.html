Subnet Calculator

Enter an IPv4 address and a CIDR prefix (or subnet mask) to calculate the network range. All calculations happen in your browser — no data is sent anywhere.

About the Subnet Calculator

A subnet calculator works out the details of an IPv4 network from an IP address and a prefix length written in CIDR notation, such as 192.168.1.10/24. The prefix (the number after the slash) says how many of the 32 address bits identify the network. The remaining bits identify hosts inside it.

What the results mean

Network address is the first address of the subnet and identifies it. Broadcast address is the last address, used to reach all hosts in the subnet. Subnet mask is the same prefix written as dotted decimal. Wildcard mask is the inverse of the mask, used in ACLs and routing protocols on many routers. Usable hosts are the addresses you can assign to devices.

Worked example: 192.168.1.10/24

Network 192.168.1.0, broadcast 192.168.1.255, mask 255.255.255.0, wildcard 0.0.0.255, usable range 192.168.1.1 to 192.168.1.254 (254 hosts).

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the number of usable hosts?

Use 2(32 − prefix) − 2. The two subtracted addresses are the network and broadcast addresses. For /26 that is 26 − 2 = 62.

What about /31 and /32?

A /31 has two addresses and is used on point-to-point links, with no separate network or broadcast address (RFC 3021). A /32 is a single host address, often used for loopbacks and host routes.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. All calculations run in your browser, and nothing is stored or transmitted.
