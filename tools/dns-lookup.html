DNS Lookup

Look up the DNS records of any domain: A, AAAA, MX, TXT, NS, CNAME, SOA and CAA. Queries are sent from your browser to Google Public DNS. This site stores nothing.

Powered by dns.google, Google's free public DNS-over-HTTPS service. It caches answers for the record's TTL, so a change you made a few minutes ago may take a while to show here.

About the DNS Lookup tool

DNS (the Domain Name System) turns names such as example.com into the records that computers need: IP addresses, mail servers, name servers and more. A DNS lookup shows those records for a domain. It is one of the first things to check when a website does not load, when email is not arriving, or after you have changed DNS settings at your registrar.

Record types

TypeWhat it holds
AThe IPv4 address of the host
AAAAThe IPv6 address of the host
CNAMEAn alias that points one name to another name
MXThe mail servers for the domain, each with a priority (lower is tried first)
NSThe authoritative name servers for the domain
TXTFree text, used for SPF, DKIM, DMARC and domain ownership verification
SOAStart of Authority: the primary name server, admin contact and zone timers
CAAWhich certificate authorities may issue SSL certificates for the domain

What TTL means

TTL (time to live) is the number of seconds a resolver may cache a record. After you change a record, resolvers that cached the old one keep serving it until its TTL runs out. This is why a change can take minutes or hours to appear everywhere.

Command-line equivalents

The same lookups from a terminal: nslookup -type=MX example.com or dig MX example.com.

Frequently asked questions

Why does this show different results from my own computer?

This tool asks Google Public DNS, while your computer asks your ISP's or company's resolver. Each caches records separately, so they can disagree for a while after a change.

What does NXDOMAIN mean?

It means the domain name does not exist in DNS at all. Check the spelling, or whether the domain has expired or was never registered.

The domain exists but I get no records. Why?

That record type is simply not set for the name. For example, many domains have no AAAA record if they do not use IPv6.

How do I check my email setup?

Look up MX to see where mail is delivered, and TXT to see the SPF record (it starts with v=spf1). A DMARC policy is a TXT record on the name _dmarc. followed by your domain.

Is my search stored?

EJD Tech has no server-side code or database and stores nothing. Your browser sends the query directly to Google Public DNS, which handles it under its own privacy policy.
